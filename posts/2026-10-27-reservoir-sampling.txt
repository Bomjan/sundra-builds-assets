How do you pick one random log entry when you don’t know how many will arrive?

You don’t need to save them all.

Reservoir sampling keeps one candidate:

1. Keep the first item.
2. For the nth item, replace your candidate with probability 1/n.
3. Otherwise, keep your current pick.

At item 2, replace with probability 1/2. At item 3, use 1/3. At item 4, use 1/4.

After n items, every item seen so far has exactly a 1/n chance of being your pick, assuming independent, uniform random choices.

Why does it work? An earlier item’s chance of surviving shrinks at exactly the right rate. After three items, the first item survives with probability (1 − 1/2) × (1 − 1/3) = 1/3.

One pass. One stored item, plus a counter. No need to know the stream’s final size.

A useful technique for sampling large files or streams without loading everything into memory.
